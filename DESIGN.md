# Design System: KECHOO

## 1. Overview

**Creative North Star: “The Calibrated Cut”**

KECHOO should feel like a freshly inspected cutting edge: controlled, exact, and visibly made for work. The system translates “工艺出奇，品质出众” into disciplined composition, decisive typography, precise product photography, and evidence-led technical communication. “Choose Better Cutting” is a practical promise, so every important screen must help a buyer compare, select, verify, purchase, or request advice.

The visual language combines the confidence of established industrial brands with the clarity of a calibrated instrument panel. It is branded but never ornamental, energetic but never aggressive. It explicitly rejects the crowded marketplace-style homepage, vague factory claims, unedited or awkward English, a lowest-price-only position, decorative industrial cliches, excessive animation, generic stock-factory imagery, and product pages that expose hundreds of variations without selection guidance.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Deep, controlled red used as a deliberate brand field rather than a discount signal
- Graphite structure and cool neutral working surfaces
- Technical clarity with generous space around decisive information
- Real cutting details, material textures, testing evidence, and application photography
- Responsive interaction without theatrical motion

## 2. Colors

The palette is committed and controlled: a deep oxide red carries the identity, graphite carries authority, and cool achromatic surfaces keep specifications readable across food, wood, and metal applications.

### Primary

- **Calibrated Oxide Red** (`#B51D24`): The KECHOO signature color. Use for decisive brand fields, primary actions, active states, packaging cues, and selected technical highlights. It must read as engineered and mineral, never fluorescent or promotional.
- **Deep Cut Red** (`#8E151B`): A controlled darker red for depth, hover states, and contrast inside large brand fields.

### Secondary

- **Machine Graphite** (`#1B2226`): The structural color for navigation, primary text, diagrams, and high-authority sections.
- **Fresh-Cut Steel** (`#B9C0C2`): A restrained cool metallic neutral for technical tables, dividers, diagrams, and secondary surfaces. It must remain flat and clean, not simulated chrome.

### Neutral

- **Inspection White** (`#FFFFFF`): The main reading and product-inspection surface.
- **Cool Workshop Gray** (`#F1F3F3`): A quiet alternate surface for filters, specifications, comparison areas, and application guidance.
- **Muted Technical Ink** (`#5A6469`): Secondary copy and metadata, with WCAG 2.2 AA contrast maintained.

### Named Rules

**The Engineered Red Rule.** Red signals KECHOO, action, and verified selection. It is forbidden as a blanket sale color or a decoration scattered across every component.

**The Three-World Rule.** Food, wood, and metal applications may use distinct photography and material texture, but they always share the same red, graphite, neutral, and typographic system.

## 3. Typography

**Display Font:** Barlow, 800–900
**Body Font:** Barlow, 400–700

**Character:** One carefully chosen technical sans family creates a unified industrial voice. Wide, firm headlines express confidence; neutral text shapes and open spacing keep specifications, dimensions, and multilingual content easy to scan.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (`clamp(3rem, 5.2vw, 5.25rem)`, 800): Reserved for the primary brand statement and major application gateways. Never louder than the product imagery it introduces.
- **Headline** (`clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.6rem)`, 800): Used for product families, solution sections, and technical proof.
- **Title** (`1.25–1.75rem`, 700–800): Used for products, filters, specification groups, and buying decisions.
- **Body** (`1rem`, 400–500, `1.6` line-height): Optimized for clear technical English with a maximum line length of 65–75 characters.
- **Label** (`0.78–0.9rem`, 700): Used for dimensions, units, stock state, taxonomy, and concise interface labels. Uppercase is restricted to short labels of three words or fewer.

### Named Rules

**The One-Voice Rule.** One type family must carry brand, commerce, and technical data through deliberate changes in scale, weight, width, and spacing. Decorative type pairing is prohibited.

**The Specification Is Content Rule.** Dimensions, TPI, blade material, compatibility, lead time, and stock status must receive designed hierarchy, never be buried in paragraph text.

## 4. Elevation

The system is flat by default. Depth comes from tonal separation, spacing, crop, and clear borders around functional controls. Shadows are reserved for temporary layers such as menus, dialogs, and purchase confirmations; product cards and ordinary sections do not float.

### Named Rules

**The Workbench Rule.** Product information rests on stable surfaces. If a card looks soft, weightless, glossy, or glass-like, it is wrong for KECHOO.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** make “KECHOO — Choose Better Cutting.” the practical promise behind selection, comparison, checkout, and RFQ flows.
- **Do** use red in large, intentional moments and on high-value actions rather than as a constant accent.
- **Do** photograph real blade teeth, welds, coils, cut surfaces, workpieces, machines, testing, and packaging with controlled industrial lighting.
- **Do** lead visitors into food and bone, wood, or metal applications before presenting blade metallurgy and manufacturing categories.
- **Do** give specifications, compatibility, stock state, price, MOQ, and lead time a consistent visual hierarchy.
- **Do** maintain WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, keyboard navigation, visible focus, and reduced-motion behavior.

### Don't:

- **Don't** create a crowded marketplace-style homepage or expose hundreds of raw variations without selection guidance.
- **Don't** use vague factory claims, unedited or awkward English, or a lowest-price-only position.
- **Don't** make red look like a discount banner, warning screen, or generic power-tool brand imitation.
- **Don't** mix food, wood, and metal applications into one undifferentiated catalog.
- **Don't** use decorative industrial cliches, simulated sparks, fake chrome, hazard stripes, excessive animation, or generic stock-factory imagery.
- **Don't** rely on glassmorphism, gradient text, oversized rounded cards, soft floating shadows, or repetitive icon-card grids.
