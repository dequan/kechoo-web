# Design System: Band Saw Blade Supply

## 1. Overview

**Creative north star: The Calibrated Cut.**

Band Saw Blade Supply should feel like a freshly inspected cutting edge: controlled, exact, and visibly made for work. The system turns blade selection into a clear buyer path: compare application, confirm dimensions, review tooth pitch, then request a quotation with enough detail to avoid wrong-fit orders.

The visual language combines the confidence of established industrial suppliers with the clarity of a calibrated instrument panel. It is practical but not plain, branded but never ornamental, energetic but never aggressive. It rejects crowded marketplace pages, vague factory claims, unedited English, lowest-price-only positioning, decorative industrial cliches, excessive animation, generic stock-factory imagery, and product pages that expose raw variations without selection guidance.

**Key characteristics:**

- Deep, controlled red used as a deliberate action field rather than a discount signal
- Graphite structure and cool neutral working surfaces
- Technical clarity with generous space around decisive information
- Real cutting details, material textures, testing evidence and application photography
- Responsive interaction without theatrical motion

## 2. Colors

The palette is committed and controlled: deep oxide red carries action, graphite carries authority, and cool achromatic surfaces keep specifications readable across food, wood and metal applications.

### Primary

- **Calibrated Oxide Red** (`#B51D24`): The action color. Use for decisive brand fields, primary actions, active states, packaging cues and selected technical highlights. It must read as engineered and mineral, never fluorescent or promotional.
- **Deep Cut Red** (`#8E151B`): A controlled darker red for depth, hover states and contrast inside large action fields.

### Secondary

- **Machine Graphite** (`#1B2226`): The structural color for navigation, primary text, diagrams and high-authority sections.
- **Fresh-Cut Steel** (`#B9C0C2`): A restrained cool metallic neutral for technical tables, dividers, diagrams and secondary surfaces. It must remain flat and clean, not simulated chrome.

### Neutral

- **Inspection White** (`#FFFFFF`): The main reading and product-inspection surface.
- **Cool Workshop Gray** (`#F1F3F3`): A quiet alternate surface for filters, specifications, comparison areas and application guidance.
- **Muted Technical Ink** (`#5A6469`): Secondary copy and metadata, with WCAG 2.2 AA contrast maintained.

### Named Rules

**The Engineered Red Rule.** Red signals action and verified selection. It is forbidden as a blanket sale color or a decoration scattered across every component.

**The Three-World Rule.** Food, wood and metal applications may use distinct photography and material texture, but they always share the same red, graphite, neutral and typographic system.

## 3. Typography

**Display font:** Barlow, 800-900  
**Body font:** Barlow, 400-700

One technical sans family creates a unified industrial voice. Wide, firm headlines express confidence; neutral text shapes and open spacing keep specifications, dimensions and multilingual content easy to scan.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (`clamp(3rem, 5.2vw, 5.25rem)`, 800): Reserved for the primary statement and major application gateways. Never louder than the product imagery it introduces.
- **Headline** (`clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.6rem)`, 800): Used for product families, solution sections and technical proof.
- **Title** (`1.25-1.75rem`, 700-900): Used for products, filters, specification groups and buying decisions.
- **Body** (`1rem`, 400-700, `1.6` line-height): Optimized for clear technical English with a maximum line length of 65-75 characters.
- **Label** (`0.78-0.9rem`, 700): Used for dimensions, units, taxonomy and concise interface labels. Uppercase is restricted to short labels of three words or fewer.

### Named Rules

**The One-Voice Rule.** One type family must carry brand, commerce and technical data through deliberate changes in scale, weight, width and spacing. Decorative type pairing is prohibited.

**The Specification Is Content Rule.** Dimensions, TPI, blade material, compatibility, MOQ and lead time must receive designed hierarchy, never be buried in paragraph text.

## 4. Elevation

The system is flat by default. Depth comes from tonal separation, spacing, crop and clear borders around functional controls. Shadows are reserved for temporary layers such as menus and dialogs. Product cards and ordinary sections do not float.

### Named Rules

**The Workbench Rule.** Product information rests on stable surfaces. If a card looks soft, weightless, glossy or glass-like, it is wrong for this site.

## 5. Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Make specification, comparison and quotation the practical promise behind the site.
- Use red in large, intentional moments and on high-value actions rather than as a constant accent.
- Photograph real blade teeth, welds, coils, cut surfaces, workpieces, machines, testing and packaging with controlled industrial lighting.
- Lead visitors into food and bone, wood or metal applications before presenting blade metallurgy and manufacturing categories.
- Give specifications, compatibility, MOQ and lead time a consistent visual hierarchy.
- Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, keyboard navigation, visible focus and reduced-motion behavior.

### Don't

- Do not create a crowded marketplace-style homepage or expose hundreds of raw variations without selection guidance.
- Do not use vague factory claims, unedited English or a lowest-price-only position.
- Do not make red look like a discount banner, warning screen or generic power-tool brand imitation.
- Do not mix food, wood and metal applications into one undifferentiated catalog.
- Do not use decorative industrial cliches, simulated sparks, fake chrome, hazard stripes, excessive animation or generic stock-factory imagery.
- Do not rely on glassmorphism, gradient text, oversized rounded cards, soft floating shadows or repetitive icon-card grids.
